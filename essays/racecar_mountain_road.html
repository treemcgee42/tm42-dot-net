The exultations of the racing engine settled into resonance in her bones. That infinite song from the game of her youth: late nights tingling with the fear of monsters, inside and out. It was to that simple melody and tempo the Pacific mountain trees streaked past her. An empty highway, an ending known.

II. An empty highway, an ending known. Leaving him behind, she was driving to the edge of the world. Skating on a blade at the mountains' edge, she could feel the breath of ice in her racecar, which seemed to have more negative space than she had thought.

III. Magnification awaited her twenty-nine miles past these mountain roads and three decades later. But she did not know it all now. Nor did she know her speed, or the speed limit. Her engine hummed at the frequency of those ancient things, and nothing would matter less than it did now, the jubilee of the past.

3 variations of a racecar on a mountain road

Commentary

I was in Washington for my cousin's wedding. We were driving up to the venues, on a highway to and through various mountains, when it seemed like the other cars were fading away. Maybe it was because Formula 1 contract news was on my mind, but started hearing the roar of a racing engine. In that meditative state, I also began to hear Infinite Amethyst by Lena Raine. The interesting thing about that song is that it makes me so nostalgic, despite it being introduced to Minecraft long after I began playing it. Which only speaks to Lena's remarkable achievement.

In that nostalgic stupor, I felt as though we were driving to the edge of the world. The wedding venue was right on the Canadian border, so the feeling wasn't totally unfounded. That made me feel sad, and cold. And old.

The third variation came from the strange hope that sometimes follows homesickness. Someone I love was going through a difficult transition; I was thinking of them. It's hard to someone you care about struggle like that, especially when you have the utmost conviction that they will be successful.
